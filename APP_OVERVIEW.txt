OPTION TRADER - OVERVIEW
=======================

1. WHAT THE APP DOES
--------------------
Option Trader is an automated seller of far out-of-the-money SPX index
options (SPXW, nearest expiration). It runs continuously inside a Docker
container next to an IB Gateway (Interactive Brokers), and earns the premium
of short calls and puts that are expected to expire worthless, while
limiting the loss on the ones that don't.

The app (app/main.py) runs several supervised asyncio tasks side by side;
each is restarted automatically if it crashes or the IB connection drops:

  - ConnectionManager   Keeps the connection to IB Gateway alive.
  - OptionTrader        The main trading loop. While the market is open it
                        asks OpportunityExplorer to look for something to
                        sell, and PositionsManager to manage what is held.
  - OptionSafeguard     Watches every short position's price and closes it
                        with a buy order once it reaches its stop loss.
  - SubscriptionManager Maintains streaming market-data subscriptions
                        (SPX options and their ES futures-option
                        counterparts, used to sanity-check SPX quotes).
  - StateUpdater        Builds a snapshot of the account and positions,
                        caches it locally and posts it to the web dashboard
                        (frontend/).
  - OptionSampler       Collects training data (see section 2).
  - PredictionsEvaluator Checks, after each expiration, how well the model's
                        predictions held up (see section 4).

How a sale is decided (OpportunityExplorer + StrikeFinder):
  1. Max loss per option (MaxLossCalculator): how much the account is
     willing to lose on one contract, derived from the cash balance and how
     many contracts are / may be held. The stop loss of a new position is
     its sell price plus this amount.
  2. Target delta (TargetDeltaCalculator): the highest delta a sold option
     may have, proportional to the max loss per option (and reduced late in
     the day / overnight). Lower delta = further from the money = safer.
  3. Strike selection (StrikeFinder): fetches market snapshots for a block
     of strikes and picks the option with the highest delta that is still
     below the target delta.
     When a machine-learning slot is free ({call,put}_ml_slot_count in
     config/option_trader_config.json), it can instead pick the option with
     the highest *expected profit* according to the trained model, among
     options whose delta is at most {call,put}_max_delta. Such positions are
     marked is_ml_derived and occupy a slot until they are closed.
  4. Checks before selling: price estimate (PriceEstimator), minimum sell
     price, margin / exposure limits (NetWorthCalculator), then a limit
     sell order through TradingBot.

Expected profit of a candidate (utilities/ib_utils.py):
    expected_profit = p * sell_price - (1 - p) * max_loss_per_option
where p is the model's probability that the option's ask never reaches
the stop loss before expiration.

Runtime settings are read from config/option_trader_config.json and can be
changed without restarting (e.g. should_monitor_only, number of daily
samples, ML slot counts, max deltas).


2. HOW DATA ITEMS ARE COLLECTED
-------------------------------
Every training row describes one option at the moment it was (or could
have been) sold, plus what then happened to it. In code a row starts life
as a PositionInitialState (utilities/ib_utils.py).

Two sources of rows:

  a) Real trades (is_executed = 1)
     When OpportunityExplorer sells an option it records the option's
     state at that moment. When the sell order fills, PositionsManager
     stores it in position_initial_state_map, keyed by contract id.

  b) Random samples (is_executed = 0) - OptionSampler
     Real trades are few and all chosen by the same rule, so the app also
     records "would-be" trades that are never actually sold. Each trading
     day is divided into number_of_samples_per_day equal slices of
     open-market time (nightly breaks / holidays excluded); at each slice
     it picks a random right (C/P), a random stop loss distance (50%-150%
     of the current max loss) and a random target delta (0.75x to
     target_delta_top_multiplier x the normal one), finds the matching
     option, and records its state. This spreads the data over many more
     deltas and stop distances than real trading alone would.

What is recorded at sale/sample time (the features):
  estimated_sell_price, stop_loss, target_delta, bid/ask/last/model delta,
  gamma, vega, theta, minutes_to_expiration, atm_iv (SPX implied
  volatility), contract_iv, distance_to_strike_pct, distance_to_stop_pct.

What is filled in afterwards (the outcome):
  max_ask - the highest ask price the option reached between the moment
  it was recorded and its expiration. While the market is open, every
  incoming quote updates max_ask if it is higher (quotes flagged as unfair
  against the ES counterpart are ignored). For samples, and for positions
  that were bought back at a high price, the max ask is re-checked after
  expiration from IB historical minute bars (MarketDataFetcher.find_max_ask).

When the option has expired, the completed row is appended to
cache/close_events.csv inside the container (PositionsManager._log_close_event)
and removed from memory. Rows waiting for their expiration are kept in the
cached state file so they survive restarts.

Getting the data for training (docker/update_model.ps1, step 1):
docker/fetch_options_data.ps1 copies cache/close_events.csv from the
container on the cloud VM to machine_learning/data/options_data.csv on the
local machine.


3. MACHINE LEARNING STEPS
-------------------------
Goal: for an option about to be sold, estimate
    P(survive) = P(max_ask < stop_loss)
i.e. the probability that the ask never crosses the stop loss before
expiration (the stop is never hit). One model is selected and trained
separately for calls and for puts.

Run with docker/update_model.ps1 (fetch data -> select model -> install),
or directly: python -m app.machine_learning.select_probability_model

Step 1 - Pre-processing (pre_processing.py, select_probability_model.py)
  - Adds max_delta = the largest of the four delta readings.
  - Splits rows by right (C / P).
  - Drops rows with missing features, and rows with max_ask <= 0 or
    estimated_sell_price <= 0 (log-based transforms need positive values).
  - Label: survived = 1 if max_ask < stop_loss, else 0
    (survival_scoring.compute_survival_label).

Step 2 - Candidate model families (eight in total)
  a) Regression + residual distribution (six combinations):
     A linear regression predicts max_ask under one of three target
     transforms (probability_transforms.py):
        raw        max_ask
        log        log(max_ask)
        log_ratio  log(max_ask / estimated_sell_price)
     The prediction becomes a probability through a residual distribution
     fitted to the training residuals: "normal" or "empirical"
     (Laplace-smoothed). P(survive) = P(prediction + residual < stop).
  b) Logistic regression (logistic_survival.py):
     Predicts P(survive) directly. The stop distance enters as the feature
     log_k = log(stop / sell_price), always included. To teach the model
     how survival changes with the stop, every trade is replicated on a
     fixed grid of 15 k values (1.25 to 8.0, log spaced), each replicate
     labelled max_ask < k * sell_price and weighted 1/15. Fitted in two
     stages: log_k alone without penalty to fix its slope, then the other
     (standardised) features with L2 regularisation and log_k's
     contribution held fixed as an offset. A fit whose log_k coefficient is
     not positive (a further stop must not lower survival) is rejected.
  c) XGBoost classifier (xgboost_survival.py):
     Same k-grid replication and log_k feature as logistic; a monotone
     constraint on log_k enforces "further stop => not less likely to
     survive". The L2 strength (reg_lambda) is searched alongside features.

Step 3 - Feature selection and scoring (best_subset.py)
  - Candidate features: estimated_sell_price, the four deltas, max_delta,
    gamma, vega, theta, minutes_to_expiration, atm_iv, contract_iv,
    distance_to_strike_pct.
  - Best-subset search: subsets are tried in increasing size; the search
    stops when 4 consecutive sizes bring no improvement, or when the time
    budget (SEARCH_TIME_BUDGET_SEC) runs out.
  - Every candidate is scored by 5-fold GroupKFold cross-validation
    grouped by expiration date, so all rows of one trading day are always
    on the same side of the split (no leakage between same-day rows).
    Logistic/XGBoost fit on the grid replicates but are always scored on
    one row per trade at its real stop.
  - Score (default): weighted log loss of the out-of-fold P(survive), each
    row weighted by stop_loss - estimated_sell_price, so trades with more
    money at risk count more. Alternatives: plain log loss, or "rss".

Step 4 - Choosing the winner (select_probability_model.py)
  - All candidates of all families are pooled and the lowest score wins.
  - A standard error is computed from the winner's per-fold scores, and
    the report states how many other candidates are within one standard
    error (many = the winner is not clearly better than the rest).

Step 5 - Final fit and deployment
  - The winning (family, feature subset, distribution / regularisation) is
    refitted on all rows of that right.
  - Calls and puts models are saved together as
    machine_learning/model/best_probability_model.pkl.
  - docker/install_probability_classifier.ps1 copies the file into the
    container. Predictor (app/predictor.py) reloads it automatically when
    the file changes, and from then on every probability / expected profit
    computed by the app uses the new model.


4. CHECKING THE MODEL IN PRODUCTION
-----------------------------------
While scanning for candidates, StrikeFinder logs the best expected-profit
option to cache/predictions_<expiry>.csv (time, strike, right, sell price,
stop loss, predicted probability, expected profit). After that expiration's
session closes, PredictionsEvaluator fetches each option's minute bars
from its record time to expiration, checks whether the stop was hit, and
computes the realized profit. It writes (tab-separated, in cache/):
  - prediction_averages.csv: average expected vs. realized profit per day.
  - probability_buckets.csv: per probability bucket (0.65-0.70, ...,
    0.95-1.00) how many records fell in it and the fraction that did not
    hit the stop - i.e. whether a predicted 0.90 really survives ~90% of
    the time.
  - aggregated_probability_buckets.csv: the same rates pooled over all
    days so far, one row per day.
  - early_closing_* versions of the bucket files, restricted to records
    made during the early-closing window (16:00-16:15 New York time).
